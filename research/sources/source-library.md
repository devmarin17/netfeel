# Source Library

The provenance layer behind the weak signals: every source, how it was verified, and what it says.

**Method:** every source below was (a) verified to exist via direct fetch or 2+ independent search hits; (b) attributed precisely (authors/venue/edition); (c) mined for exact stats/quotes with original wording where fetched directly; (d) dated. Items that could not be fully verified are labeled and never load-bearing.

**Tier legend:**
- **[PRIMARY]** — company/government/institutional data owner (earnings letters, newsroom posts, ITU/Ofcom/Nielsen/WEF, primary shareholder letters)
- **[PEER-REVIEWED]** — journal articles, peer-venue papers, academic books with DOI/press verification
- **[MAJOR-PRESS]** — Reuters, BBC, Guardian, Bloomberg, Axios, Deadline, THR, TechCrunch, etc.
- **[EXPERT]** — named-credential consultancies/futurists/authors (PwC, Deloitte, McKinsey, Webb, Kelly, Kurzweil…)
- **[GREY]** — single-source/blog/aggregator; flagged and never allowed to carry a load-bearing claim

## CATEGORY 2 — Academic & peer-reviewed (the rigor layer)

### 6. Binge-watching: the first systematic review — [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Citation:** Flayelle, M., Maurage, P., Di Lorenzo, K.R., Vögele, C., Gainsbury, S.M., & Billieux, J. (2020). "Binge-Watching: What Do We Know So Far? A First Systematic Review of the Evidence." *Current Addiction Reports*, 7(1). **DOI: 10.1007/s40429-020-00299-8** (verified via Crossref; **139 citations**).
- **Takeaway:** the field's baseline synthesis: binge-watching is a widespread, mostly benign behavior with a **minority pathological subgroup**; drivers are engagement/immersion more than escape — evidence against casual "binge = addiction" framing, and for designing *healthy-session* mechanics. (This is the review to cite before any wellbeing claim.)
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s40429-020-00299-8

### 7. Binge-viewing vs. sleep — the classic empirical study — [PEER-REVIEWED]
- **Citation:** Exelmans, L., & Van den Bulck, J. (2017). "Binge Viewing, Sleep, and the Role of Pre-Sleep Arousal." *Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine*, 13(8), 1001–1008. **DOI: 10.5664/jcsm.6704** (verified via Crossref; **147 citations**).
- **Takeaway:** in 18–25s, more binge-viewing → **poorer sleep quality, more insomnia symptoms, more fatigue — mediated by pre-sleep cognitive arousal** ("just one more episode" as arousal loop). The physiological mechanism behind every phone-ban/sleep argument.
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.5664/jcsm.6704

### 8. Attention-span / task-switching research — [PEER-REVIEWED + EXPERT]
- **Citations:**
  - Mark, G., Gudith, D., & Klocke, U. (2008). "The Cost of Interrupted Work: More Speed and Stress." *Proc. CHI 2008*. **DOI: 10.1145/1357054.1357072** (verified via Crossref; **547 citations**). Finding: interrupted work is performed faster but at the cost of **higher stress and frustration** — people compensate with speed, not quality.
  - Mark, G. (2023). *Attention Span: A Groundbreaking Way to Restore Balance, Happiness and Productivity.* Hanover Square Press (book verified via Open Library, first published 2023). Her longitudinal screens-work finding: average attention on any screen task fell from **2.5 minutes (2004) to ~47 seconds (recent years)** — the "47-second attention" statistic, widely cited in press coverage of the book.
- **Takeaway:** the scientific case that *self-interruption + notification ecosystems*, not content length, drive attention fragmentation. Kids' 73%-long-form YouTube behavior (see the kids-media brief) is consistent: **long attention is possible when the loop is intrinsically rewarding.**
- **URLs:** https://doi.org/10.1145/1357054.1357072 · book: https://openlibrary.org/works/OL27804591W

### 9. Parasocial relationships with AI companions — [PEER-REVIEWED]

- **Takeaway:** the method spine for a 2046 scenario: pick one Dator archetype, build it Schwartz-style as a story, and let the ridiculousness be the point ("Dator's law" is also the license for the weirdest signals).
