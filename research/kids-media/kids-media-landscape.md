## 1. Where they consume

### 1.1 Platform hierarchy: YouTube first, everything else distant

**US teens, platform use** ([Pew 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2024/12/12/teens-social-media-and-technology-2024/), N=1,391; [Pew 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/12/09/teens-social-media-and-ai-chatbots-2025/), N=1,458):

| Platform | Use (2024) | Daily use (2025) | "Almost constantly" (2025) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **YouTube** | **90%** | **73%** (Pew Dec 2024; most of any platform) | ~19–20% |
| TikTok | 63% | 61% | 21% (up from 16% in 2022) |
| Instagram | 61% | 55% | 12% |
| Snapchat | 55% | 46% | 12% |
| Facebook | 32% (was 71% in 2014-15) | 20% | — |
| WhatsApp | 23% (up from 17% in 2022) | — | — |
| X / Reddit / Threads | 17% / 14% / 6% | — | — |

- **36% of teens use at least one platform "almost constantly"** (Pew 2025). YouTube is the only platform used equally by younger and older teens.
- Splits matter: boys > girls on YouTube (94% vs 89%); Black teens 35% and Hispanic 23% on YouTube almost constantly vs White 8%; TikTok almost-constantly: Black 28%, Hispanic 25%, White 8% (Pew 2024/2025).

### 1.2 The TV set is back — but it's YouTube's TV set

- **YouTube is the #1 media company on the TV**: 12.4% of all US TV watch time (Apr 2025) → **13.4% (Jul 2025)** — six consecutive months at #1, its largest-ever lead over #2 Disney (9.4%) ([Nielsen](https://www.nielsen.com/news-center/2025/youtube-netflix-ride-the-wave-of-summer-streaming-highs-in-nielsens-media-distributor-gauge/)). Netflix's record: 9.0% (Dec 2025).
- **Streaming passed broadcast + cable combined** in May 2025 (44.8% of US TV time), reaching **47.5% by Dec 2025**; cable is down to ~20% ([Nielsen](https://www.nielsen.com/news-center/2025/nielsens-the-gauge-broadcast-and-streaming-power-historic-month/)).
- UK mirror image ([Ofcom Media Nations 2025](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/media-habits-adults/tuning-into-youtube-uks-media-habits-revealed)): YouTube is now the **2nd most-watched media service in the UK, behind only the BBC**; the TV set became the first-choice YouTube device for 16–34s in Dec 2024.
- For UK kids, broadcast TV is functionally dead: **2h32min of live broadcast TV per week** for ages 4–15 in 2025 ([Ofcom Children & Parents 2025-6](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/media-literacy-research/children/2026-children-and-parents-report/children-and-parents-media-use-and-attitudes-report-2025-6.pdf), Barb data).
- Studios are chasing the kids: Warner Bros Discovery put **31 full-length movies free on YouTube** (Feb 2025, [Variety](https://variety.com/2025/digital/news/warner-bros-free-movies-youtube-streaming-1236298880/)).

### 1.3 Devices by age

- US teens' access ([Pew 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2024/12/12/teens-social-media-and-technology-2024/)): smartphone **95%**, computer 88%, gaming console 83%, tablet 70%.
- UK 3–17s online ([Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/online-safety/research-statistics-and-data/protecting-children/childrens-online-experiences-research-report.pdf), 2025): mobile phone most-used device **77%**, tablet 59%, console 55%.
- It starts early ([Common Sense 0–8 Census 2025](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/the-2025-common-sense-census-media-use-by-kids-zero-to-eight)): **40% of children have their own tablet by age 2**; ~1 in 4 have a cellphone by age 8; 72% of UK 3–5s use tablets as their primary device (Ofcom 2025).

### 1.4 Gaming platforms are media venues

- **Roblox**: 151.5M DAU in Q3 2025 (+70% YoY; users 13+ grew 89%); **39.6B hours engaged in a single quarter**; 124B hours and $6.8B bookings in 2025 — and **$1.5B paid out to creators** ([Roblox shareholder letters](https://s27.q4cdn.com/984876518/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/Q4-2025-Shareholder-Letter.pdf); [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/roblox-had-151m-daily-active-users-in-q3-2025/)). Roughly 35–41% of DAU is under 13.
- **In-game events = must-watch-live TV**: Travis Scott "Astronomical" (Fortnite, Apr 2020) hit **12.3M concurrent players**, 27.7M uniques ([Variety](https://variety.com/2020/digital/news/travis-scott-fortnite-record-viewers-live-1234589033/)); Lil Nas X on Roblox (Nov 2020): 33M visits across 4 shows ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2020/11/16/21570454/lil-nas-x-roblox-concert-33-million-views)). Older, but still the canonical baselines.
- Kids watch others play: **17% of US teens use Twitch (30% of teen gamers); 28% use Discord (44% of teen gamers)** ([Pew 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2024/05/09/teens-and-video-games-today/)).

### 1.5 Audio: the kids' podcast boom

- **46% of US kids 6–12 have listened to a podcast**; 29% in the last month; **68% co-listen with a parent** ([Edison Research / Kids Listen, 2023](https://www.edisonresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Kids-Podcast-Listener-Report-webinar-deck-Exporting-most-updated.pdf)).
- UK: **42.3% of 8–18s enjoy listening to audio in free time vs 34.6% who enjoy reading — listening overtook reading for the first time** ([National Literacy Trust, 2024](https://literacytrust.org.uk/research-services/research-reports/children-and-young-peoples-listening-in-2024/)).

### 1.6 Free beats paid

- **>50% of Gen Z and millennials canceled a subscription service in the past 6 months** (Deloitte 2025) — free, algorithmically endless social video is the competition ([Deloitte Digital Media Trends 2025](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/digital-media-trends-consumption-habits-survey/2025.html)).

## 2. What they consume

### 2.1 Short-form dominance — with a long-form twist

- The long-form counter-trend is the headline: **73% of ALL US YouTube watch time goes to videos 30+ minutes** (Oct 2024, up from 65% a year earlier); for US 18–24s, long-form's share of YouTube watch time rose **58% → 79%** in one year, and they average **2.7 hrs/day on YouTube across ~25 videos** ([Digital i via Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2025/02/06/data-long-form-content-takes-73-of-youtube-us-viewing-time/); [TV Technology](https://www.tvtechnology.com/news/long-form-content-viewing-on-youtube-rose-8-percent-in-2024)).
- Gen Z watches hours-long video essays and documentaries about their passions ([Think with Google, ~2024](https://business.google.com/us/think/consumer-insights/gen-z-long-form-videos/)); half of YouTube's top-trending videos now resemble traditional TV — long interviews, game shows ([Ofcom 2025](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/media-habits-adults/tuning-into-youtube-uks-media-habits-revealed)).
- ⚠️ TikTok usage stats (~54 min/day; 82% never search, just scroll the FYP; 71% open 5+ times/day) are third-party aggregations — directional only.
- **Implication:** kids don't have "short attention spans" so much as **creator-filtered attention** — they binge 3-hour video essays about Minecraft lore or F1, but discover them via clips, Shorts and the algorithm.

### 2.2 The activity mix behind the screen hours

- US teens 13–18 ([Common Sense Census 2021](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/the-common-sense-census-media-use-by-tweens-and-teens-2021)): TV/videos **3:16/day**, gaming **1:46**, social media **1:27**, browsing ~0:51, reading ~0:34 (falling).
- Under-8s ([CSM 2025](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/the-2025-common-sense-census-media-use-by-kids-zero-to-eight)): gaming time **+65% since 2020**; daily reading among 5–8s fell from 64% (2017) to **52%**; ~1/3 of parents say their young child has used AI for learning.
- Reading is in structural decline: only **34.6% of UK 8–18s enjoy reading — the lowest since measurement began in 2005** and the steepest year-on-year drop recorded ([NLT 2024](https://literacytrust.org.uk/research-services/research-reports/children-and-young-peoples-reading-in-2024/)); US NAEP shows double-digit drops in 9- and 13-year-olds reading for fun almost daily.

### 2.3 "Brain rot" and the attention debate

- **"Brain rot" = Oxford Word of the Year 2024** (37,000 votes; usage +230%) — the mainstream named its own media anxiety ([OUP](https://corp.oup.com/news/brain-rot-named-oxford-word-of-the-year-2024/)).
- Academic counter-reading: a SAGE paper argues brain-rot content (Skibidi Toilet etc.) is a *genre of participation and identity* for teens, not just cognitive damage — every generation gets a media panic ([New Media & Society, 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14614448251351527)).
- Mental health: US teens average **~4.8 hrs/day on social media** (Gallup via [APA Monitor 2024](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2024/04/teen-social-use-mental-health)); among the heaviest users, **41% rate their mental health poor/very poor** vs 11% of light users (Gallup 2023). Haidt's *The Anxious Generation* (2024) drove the policy wave; 2025 meta-analyses find only small associations — the causal debate is unresolved.

### 2.4 Algorithm-first, choice-second

- Discovery of shows/movies increasingly happens **inside social feeds** — creators and social platforms are a primary driver of viewers to TV shows and games (Deloitte 2024/2025). Gen Z treats the feed as the EPG.
- The unit of choice is the **session, not the title**: kids binge long-form through algorithmic chains (autoplay, FYP). Intentional "I'll watch X at 8pm" is the exception for this cohort.

## 3. How attention works

- **Total volume:** US teens average ~8.5–9 hrs/day of entertainment screen media ([CSM 2021](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/research/the-common-sense-census-media-use-by-tweens-and-teens-2021) — aging but still the only full census).
- **Ambient presence:** 46% of teens were online "almost constantly" (Dec 2024; a "~40% in 2025" figure circulates but is unconfirmed in Pew's own release); **53–58% of Black and Hispanic teens**, roughly 2x White teens ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2024/12/12/teens-social-media-and-technology-2024/)).
- **Snack-binge hybrid:** autoplay/FYP chains mean the session — not the episode — is the scheduling unit.
- **Second screening** is long-documented (Gen Z/millennials multitask on ~half of TV viewing) ⚠️ — no fresh 2025 number secured; cite Deloitte 2022–2024 editions if used.
- **Co-viewing survives:** 68% of kid podcast listening is co-listening with family ([Edison 2023](https://www.edisonresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Kids-Podcast-Listener-Report-webinar-deck-Exporting-most-updated.pdf)); Discord screenshares and watch parties are native teen-gamer behavior.
- **Sleep pays the bill:** **41% of teen gamers say gaming hurts their sleep** ([Pew 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2024/05/09/teens-and-video-games-today/)) — a justification behind the phone bans below.

## 4. What they DO with media — participatory culture

*This is the heart of the brief. Today's kids are not an audience. They are a labor force, a fandom, and a cast.*

> **📈 UPDATE (Oct 2026) — fandom now has numbers:** Deloitte *Digital Media Trends 2026* ("Capturing Always-On Fandom"): **~80% of consumers** self-identify as fans of at least one entertainment category; fans spend **+51 min/day (+16%)** with media vs non-fans and **$71/mo vs $56** on SVOD; **55% engage cross-platform** (≈**70%** among Gen Z/millennial fans); **46%** seek fandom content from creators. [Deloitte DMT 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/digital-media-trends-consumption-habits-survey.html) — cite the **2026 edition**, not the 19th (2025), for fandom numbers.

### 4.1 Creating is the default ambition

- **32% of US kids 12–15 want to be YouTubers; 21% want to be TikTok creators** (Whop survey of 910 kids, 2024, via [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91285945/gen-alpha-dream-job-youtube-content-creator)); **53% of 8–12s want to be content creators** ([YPulse, 2025](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2025/05/01/gen-alphas-dream-career-being-a-content-creator/)). Baseline: the LEGO 2019 survey found 1/3 of 8–12s already aspired to vlogging ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/08/02/forget-law-school-these-kids-want-to-be-a-youtube-star.html)). 1 in 3 Americans 18–30 want to be influencers (Adobe, 2024).
- **Roblox paid $1.5B to creators in 2025** — kids and teens are literally the studio labor force of the biggest kid platform ([Roblox](https://s27.q4cdn.com/984876518/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/Q4-2025-Shareholder-Letter.pdf)).
- Remix is the literacy: CapCut fan edits, meme formats, sound reuse are the default grammar of TikTok/IG teens (Ofcom Children's Media Lives; SAGE 2025). ⚠️ No single hard % for "teens who edit video" — qualitative but overwhelming consensus.
- Fanfic at scale: AO3 hosts millions of works across ~60,000 fandoms, with a large under-25 authorship population (⚠️ self-selected [2024 demographics survey](https://archiveofourown.org/series/3911296), 16,131 respondents).

### 4.2 Fandom as identity — and the inverted pipeline

- **Skibidi Toilet: 65B+ cumulative YouTube views — and it's being turned into a film-and-TV "universe" by Michael Bay and Adam Goodman** (announced 2024–25). Kid-made UGC lore now inverts the studio pipeline: the playground sells to Hollywood ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/danidiplacido/2025/05/21/its-official-michael-bay-will-direct-the-skibidi-toilet-movie/); [CBR](https://www.cbr.com/michael-bay-skibidi-toilet-movie-info-inspiration/)).
- Anime and K-pop fandoms operate as **primary identity infrastructures** — Discord servers, edit accounts, fan art (qualitative consensus; Ofcom Media Lives).
- In Roblox's avatar economy, skins and emotes are self-expression and social currency; original-character (OC) culture thrives there (Roblox earnings calls).

### 4.3 Parasocial bonds and AI companions

- **72% of US teens have used AI companions; 52% use them a few times a month; 33% use them for social interaction and relationships** — conversation practice, emotional support, role-play, friendship, romance. ~1 in 3 have discussed something serious with an AI rather than a real person ([Common Sense Media, July 2025, N=1,060](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/nearly-3-in-4-teens-have-used-ai-companions-new-national-survey-finds)).
- Chatbots broadly: **64% of teens use AI chatbots; ChatGPT 59%, Gemini 23%, Meta AI 20%, Character.AI ~14%** (skewing to lower-income teens); 16% use a chatbot several times a day ([Pew, Dec 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/12/09/teens-social-media-and-ai-chatbots-2025/)).
- Streamers occupy the best-friend slot previous generations gave TV stars — "comfort creator" watching is a recognized self-soothing pattern (qualitative). ⚠️ No census-grade stat.

### 4.4 Social viewing is the native mode

- **89% of teen gamers play WITH other people; 47% have made a friend through games** ([Pew 2024](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2024/05/09/teens-and-video-games-today/)).
- **Discord is the de facto living room**: 28% of all teens, 44% of teen gamers — watch-alongs and screenshares of movies, streams and TikTok compilations are native behaviors there.
- In-game events are the new must-see-live TV: Travis Scott 12.3M concurrents (2020); Lil Nas X 33M Roblox visits (2020); Roblox's largest experiences now each top 10M DAU ([Roblox Q3 2025](https://gamesbeat.com/roblox-had-151m-daily-active-users-in-q3-2025/)).

## 5. Regulation & backlash

### 5.1 Australia's under-16 ban (world first — in force)

- **Since 10 December 2025**, age-restricted social media platforms must prevent under-16s from holding accounts: Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Snapchat, X, YouTube, Reddit, Twitch, Threads, Kick. Penalties up to **AUD $49.5M**. YouTube was ultimately included (despite an expected education exemption); streaming services and gaming platforms are currently out of scope. Early enforcement is imperfect; VPN workarounds reported ([eSafety Commissioner](https://www.esafety.gov.au/about-us/industry-regulation/social-media-age-restrictions); [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cwyp9d3ddqyo)).

### 5.2 EU: DSA minors regime

- **14 July 2025**: the European Commission published **guidelines on protection of minors under the Digital Services Act (Art. 28)** plus a prototype EU age-verification app; "safe by design" defaults expected to harden into enforcement through 2026+ ([EC digital strategy](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors); [age verification](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/eu-age-verification)).

### 5.3 US: KOSA and state laws

- **KOSA passed the Senate 91–3 in July 2024 but stalled in the House**; still advancing/stalling through 2025–26 — **not law** as of this pass ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/05/kosa-privacy-social-media-senate.html)). State age-verification/parental-consent laws (Utah, Texas SCOPE Act, California AADC litigation) continue piecemeal; COPPA 2.0 not enacted ⚠️ (status fluid — verify before final use).
- **School phone bans:** **114 education systems (~58% of monitored countries) ban phones in schools as of early 2026, up from 24% in June 2023** ([UNESCO GEM](https://www.unesco.org/gem-report/en/articles/phone-bans-schools-are-spreading-worldwide-policy-debate-rages)); **34 US states** had K-12 phone laws/policies by Sept 2025, including New York's bell-to-bell mandate ([Rockefeller Institute](https://rockinst.org/blog/new-york-and-other-states-enact-more-school-cell-phone-bans-restrictions/)).

### 5.4 Backlash culture — the kids' own ambivalence

- The "dumbphone revival" is press-level anecdote ⚠️, not yet market data — treat as mood, not stat.
- Haidt's *The Anxious Generation* (2024) became both bestseller and policy accelerant; the scientific counter-coalition (2025 meta-analyses) says evidence for large harms is weak. Both camps agree the environment changed radically since 2010 ([Platformer](https://www.platformer.news/anxious-generation-jonathan-haidt-debate-critique/)).
- Teens self-police: **38% say they've deliberately cut back gaming time** (Pew 2024); 38–46% say they spend too much time on their smartphone (Pew 2023). Their own vocabulary — "brain rot," "doomscrolling," "log off" — shows they love and distrust the feed simultaneously.

### 5.5 Kids prefer creators over brands and studios

- **56% of Gen Z say social content is more relevant to them than traditional TV or movies** ([Deloitte 2025](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/technology/digital-media-trends-consumption-habits-survey/2025.html)). The inversion is visible everywhere: Skibidi Toilet (65B views) → Michael Bay movie; MrBeast → Amazon game show; Roblox creators paid $1.5B/yr. **Today's kids don't dream of being in movies; they dream of owning a channel.**

## 6. Why this audience matters for Netflix in 2046

Today's 8–18-year-olds will be **~28–38 in 2046 — the heart of the core subscriber base**. Six takeaways for the concept:

1. **The TV is dead; long live the TV.** Kids' living-room screen time already flows to YouTube (13.4% of all US TV time) and games; broadcast is ~2.5 hrs/WEEK for UK kids. By 2046 the walled catalogue will feel as alien to this cohort as broadcast TV does today. **Netflix's real 2046 competitor is the algorithmic feed, not other streamers.**
2. **They are participants, not an audience.** 72% used AI companions, 41% game daily, a third of tweens want creator careers, and $1.5B/yr flows to Roblox creators. Assume viewers who co-create, remix and *inhabit* stories — watch-and-play, not watch-only.
3. **Attention is algorithmic and ambient — but not shallow.** 73% of YouTube watch time is 30+ minutes; kids binge multi-hour lore essays. Short-form is the discovery layer; long-form/immersive is the payoff layer. Design "snack → saga" pipelines with the feed as guide.
4. **Parasocial + AI is the new co-viewing.** Comfort creators, Discord watch parties and AI companions (72% adoption) mean the 2046 audience may expect characters and stories to be conversational, always-on and personally loyal — with all the ethical weight that carries.
5. **They are the regulated generation.** Australia's under-16 ban, EU DSA minors guidelines, phone bans in 58% of countries, and "brain rot" as Word of the Year: this cohort grew up inside a backlash against the feeds that raised them — they may hunger for intentional, shared, "worth-it" media moments precisely because of it.
6. **Free beats paid for this cohort** (>50% churn a subscription every 6 months; 56% find social content more relevant). A 2046 Netflix proposition must justify payment through **participation, identity and live/social features — not catalogue size.**
