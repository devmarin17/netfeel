## What this interview signals about Netflix's direction (5 bullets)

- **Entertainment isn't troubled — disaggregation favors Netflix.** Sarandos frames consumer-first disruption as Netflix's home turf ("consumers are speaking loud and clear that they prefer streaming"), with legacy media handicapped by "roots in old legacy media models."
- **AI is a creator tool and a discovery tool — not a content engine.** The doctrine: "a tool for creators to create content, not for Netflix to create instead of creators," judged by one test — "can this use of this tool make a better show?" Near-term, he expects AI's "profound impact" in the UI: helping people choose.
- **Engagement is the north star, and pricing follows value.** One-click cancel is the feedback loop; "no better measurement of joy has been better than engagement"; models that charge without engagement "feel like a hack versus a business." This is why subscriber counts stopped mattering (see [experiments.md](experiments.md), Pattern 8).
- **Live events, not leagues; formats, not platforms.** "We are in the live events business. Some of those events are sports" — event economics beat full-season economics. Games are "a better version of interactive television" inside storytelling universes; Netflix House is scaled fandom ("more important… that it's big than that it's wildly profitable").
- **The culture *is* the strategy.** Improving-not-preserving culture, keeper test retained, co-CEO model, learning-not-fear: "Don't make the same mistake a thousand times, but go ahead and make a thousand mistakes and we'll be fine." And the 1999 vision ("all home entertainment will come into the home on the internet") shows how Netflix commits to decade-scale bets — the template for any 2046 argument.

## Key quotes by theme (verbatim from the transcript above)

### The future of entertainment / streaming
- "I think the entertainment landscape is not troubled. I think the way that people consume it has become disintermediated... they're favoring things that favor the consumer, which I think does play into our strength as a company."
- "that director Reed Hastings told me in 1999, was that eventually all home entertainment is going to come into the home on the internet... it was a huge, big vision, which could have been perceived as a crazy one. But what we saw was the power of the internet for convenience, for choice, for control."
- "that was always the vision for the business, was to be a digital entertainment business. A global digital entertainment business with lots of choice and lots of selection, lots of freedom."
- "consumers are speaking loud and clear that they prefer streaming."
- "we're not just exporting Hollywood content around the world. It's not like we're doing a better version of something else. We're doing something that's really never been done before."
- "at 10% of TV engagement that feels so small, right?... what's it feel like to get to 15, to get to 20?... when we were at two, 4% seemed impossible."
- "$600 billion consumer spend [movies, TV, ad-supported content, games]... today we get about 5-6% of that. And engagement time on the TV... in the US we get about 10% of that."

### AI
- "I look at Gen AI as a tool for creators to create content, not for Netflix to create instead of creators."
- "If it passes the big test, which is can this use of this tool make a better show? Then it will be really transformative and be helpful. But if it doesn't, I think it's gonna be part of somewhere in the hype cycle of what it is right now."
- "I think it'd be very helpful in the UI in choosing... It will have a profound impact on helping people find things... gen AI to help things that are already working work better."
- On licensing gen AI for production: "I don't see us doing the work in that way... I think those tools will be widely available."

### Ads
- "it will grow slowly over the next couple of years... doubling our upfront commitments from year on year... We're building our ad teams from scratch, our sales teams from scratch. We're building new ad tech from scratch."
- "we have about 40 million users on the ad tier now and growing pretty quickly... the proven business model of video advertising... that's all we have to do is be really good at all the things to get there."
- "having an ad tier at all is just a way of opening up a larger total adjustable market to folks who don't mind advertising and want a lower price. So I just think to us, it expands the total market."

### Pricing / engagement
- "it's a value equation. So the more time you spend watching Netflix, the more you value the things you watch on Netflix... we have a very instant feedback model with consumers where one click cancel."
- "I've never seen anybody turn off a show that they love unless they just have to go to bed... And I've never seen one stick around for a show that they hate."
- "when we release a new movie, we release a new TV show, it's now competing with everything ever made, ever."
- "it feels like a hack versus a business." (on models that charge without engagement)

### YouTube / TikTok / next generation
- "in some ways it's gonna be a training ground for storytellers and creators, which is great. In other ways, it's a way that people kill time. I do think killing time and spending time are two different activities."
- "a lot of what's happening on social media and through user generated... the monetization model... isn't possible to do something at the scope and scale that we do on Netflix through that model."

### Games
- "there's a world where you can build a gaming universe that's within the storytelling universe of a show like Outer Banks... people will in between seasons... spend time in that world. And for super fans, you may even be able to spin up storylines and introduce new characters... through a game."
- "It's a better version of interactive television than we've done before."

### Sports / live
- "I'll never say never, but I would say that right now we are in the live events business. Some of those events are sports... the Tom Brady Roast is as much live event as the Tyson/Paul fight."
- "there's something very interesting and very exciting about when the world comes together to watch the same thing at the same time. They don't do it very often. That's what makes it so special."
- "the difference between the event and the full season is really the economics... distribution's pretty low margin business in league sports... I do think if we can add some value back to them, which I think we do uniquely, we can make that work."
