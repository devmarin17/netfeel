# Netflix Culture — How the Company Actually Works

> Compiled from Netflix's own culture memo and careers pages, HBR, Variety, WSJ, NYT/BBC, Reuters and more — all quotes verbatim with source URLs. Siblings: [what-netflix-does.md](what-netflix-does.md) · [experiments.md](experiments.md) · [ceo-interview.md](ceo-interview.md)

**Why this file matters:** any 2046 concept for Netflix has to survive people trained by this culture to ask: *where's the data, what would we learn if it fails, and can we kill it cheaply?* (See the closing section.)

## 0. The document's history in one paragraph

In August 2009, co-founder Reed Hastings published a ~125-slide "culture deck" (Patty McCord, chief talent officer 1998–2012, co-author). It went viral — viewed 5M+ times — and Sheryl Sandberg called it "one of the most important documents ever to come out of Silicon Valley" (McCord, "How Netflix Reinvented HR," HBR, Jan 2014 — https://hbr.org/2014/01/how-netflix-reinvented-hr). Netflix has rewritten the memo several times; the latest major rewrite published **June 24, 2024**, built by "farming for dissent" with 1,500+ employee comments (https://about.netflix.com/news/sharing-our-latest-culture-memo). Living memo: https://jobs.netflix.com/culture. Book version: Hastings & Meyer, *No Rules Rules* (2020) — https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/606529/no-rules-rules-by-reed-hastings-and-erin-meyer/

Sarandos on the origin (WSJ Tech Live, Oct 2024 — full transcript in [ceo-interview.md](ceo-interview.md)): *"instead of hiring people then telling 'em what we believe, why don't we tell people we believe and see if they wanna work here? And so he just literally one day pushed a button and put the culture memo on the internet."*

## 1. The four core principles (2024 memo, verbatim)

Source: https://jobs.netflix.com/culture

### 1.1 The Dream Team
> "We aim only to have high performers at Netflix — people who are great at what they do, and even better at working together."

**Plain language:** a sports team, not a family. You're picked for performance in your position — and swapped out if a better player exists. Excellence is the perk.

### 1.2 People Over Process
> "You get better outcomes when employees have the information and freedom to make decisions for themselves. We hire unusually responsible people who thrive on this openness and freedom."

**Plain language:** minimal rules and approvals; hire adults who don't need a policy to do the right thing. (This replaced the old "Freedom & Responsibility" heading — see §2.)

### 1.3 Uncomfortably Exciting
> "To entertain the world, we need to be bold and ambitious. That means embracing the thrill of what's next — even when it's uncomfortable."

**Plain language:** perpetual change is the job description. If you want stability, this is the wrong company.

### 1.4 Great and Always Better
> "We often say Netflix sucks today compared to where we can be tomorrow. We need the self-awareness to understand what should be better, and the discipline and resilience to get there."

**Plain language:** permanent dissatisfaction as identity — perpetual beta, applied to the company itself.

*The eight "values we value": Selflessness, Judgment, Candor, Creativity, Courage, Inclusion, Curiosity, Resilience.* (2024 memo)

## 2. Freedom & Responsibility — and its 2024 evolution

- Legacy framing (2009 deck → 2020 book): **"freedom and responsibility"** (internally "F&R"). Hastings on why few controls beat Blockbuster: "a culture that valued people over process, emphasized innovation over efficiency, and had very few controls." (Variety: https://variety.com/2020/digital/news/netflix-reed-hastings-book-five-takeaways-no-rules-rules-1234752550/)
- **2024 change:** the F&R heading was retired in favor of "unusually responsible people" inside People Over Process. Sarandos: *"the freedom, responsibility, evolution, we didn't get throw it out... we think you have to have both... the core values in the new document are all in the old document."* Also: *"we put no energy in preserving the culture. We are constantly working on improving the culture... We want it to reflect how we work, not dictate how we work."*
- The press noticed two removals: the F&R section, and the "brilliant jerks" line (gone from the 2024 memo — https://jobs.netflix.com/culture). The decency clause remains: *"No matter how brilliant someone may be, there's no place in our Dream Team for people who don't treat their colleagues with decency and respect."*

**Design-futures hook:** culture as a product Netflix iterates like software — "we constantly seek to improve our culture, not preserve it" (2024 memo).

## 3. Dream Team, "not a family," and top-of-market pay

- Team not family (2024 memo): *"we model ourselves on a professional sports team, not a family. Families are about unconditional love. They can also be dysfunctional... Professional sports teams... focus on performance and picking the right person for every position, even when that means swapping out someone they love for a better player."*
- Bar for talent: *"a high performer in any role is many times more effective than the average employee, our Dream Team is driven by performance — not seniority, tenure or unconditional loyalty."* (2024 memo)
- **Top-of-market pay** (official): *"we pay employees at the top of their personal market... there is no raise pool to divide up... If your market adjusts, we do not have to wait for an annual compensation event to make changes."* — https://jobs.netflix.com/work-life-philosophy
- Employees choose salary vs. stock options each year ("all cash, all options, or whatever combination suits you"). Same source.
- Managers are told to know each person's market value; employees are encouraged to collect poaching offers as market data. Hastings: *"It costs a lot more to lose people and to recruit replacements than to overpay a little in the first place."* (Variety)
- Caveat: "personal top of market" is deliberately judgment-based, not banded (2024 memo); critics read the model as brutal churn dressed as generosity. Hastings concedes F&R has limits — safety, harassment, privacy, financial reporting are "where error prevention is clearly more important than innovation" (Variety).

## 4. The Keeper Test

- Official (2024 memo): managers ask *"'if X wanted to leave, would I fight to keep them?' Or 'knowing everything I know today, would I hire X again?' If the answer is no, we believe it's fairer to everyone to part ways quickly."*
- Name origin: Hastings caught a fish as a child; his dad said, "That's a keeper, Reed!" (2024 memo footnote)
- Origin story: in the 2001 dot-com bust Netflix laid off 1/3 of its 120-person staff — and morale *soared*: *"the entire office felt like it was filled with people who were madly in love with their work"* — Hastings' "road to Damascus moment." (Variety, *No Rules Rules* takeaways)
- Sarandos defending it (WSJ, Oct 2024): *"it is often written about as being this kind of cold and cutthroat thing and it's really not. It's just are we constantly saying, are we happy with each other?... being able to be candid with each other and ask the hard question."* And asked if it's rigidly applied: *"It's still in there... Yeah."*
- The infamous corollary: *"At Netflix, adequate performance gets a generous severance and hard work is irrelevant."* (https://www.norulesrules.com/)
- Applied at the top: CCO Jonathan Friedland was fired in June 2018 after using the N-word twice at work. Sources: https://www.nytimes.com/2018/06/22/business/media/jonathan-friedland-netflix-racial-slur.html ; https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-44585072

## 5. Candor

- 2024 memo: *"we practice extraordinary candor — ensuring constructive feedback is part of our everyday work (like brushing your teeth)... and integrity only to say things about a colleague you're willing to share with them directly."*
- Origin: Hastings learned it in couples' therapy during his Pure Software years — "I began encouraging everyone to say exactly what they really thought, but with positive intent." (Variety)
- Institutionalized after the Qwikster fiasco: *"it is disloyal to Netflix when you disagree with an idea and do not express that disagreement."* (See §8.2 and the operational story in [experiments.md](experiments.md))
- HBR tagline (McCord, 2014): *"Trust people, not policies. Reward candor. And throw away the standard playbook."* (https://hbr.org/2014/01/how-netflix-reinvented-hr)

## 6. Context, not Control — informed captains, farming for dissent

All verbatim from the 2024 memo (https://jobs.netflix.com/culture):

- *"We expect managers to practice context not control — giving their teams the context and clarity needed to make good decisions instead of trying to control everything themselves."*
- *"It takes an unusually responsible person to thrive on this level of freedom — someone who's self-motivated, self-aware and self-disciplined, who doesn't wait to be told what to do and picks up the trash like they would at home."*
- *"We avoid decision-making by committee... For every significant decision, we identify an informed captain who's responsible for making a judgment call... highly aligned and loosely coupled."*
- *"We call this farming for dissent... After a decision is made, we expect everyone... to disagree then commit."*
- On leverage: senior leaders pride themselves on *"how few, not how many, decisions"* they make.

## 7. The (almost) no-rules rules

- Vacation — two words: **"Take vacation."** Expenses — five words: **"Act in Netflix's best interests."** Parental leave: **"Take care of your child and yourself."** (2024 memo; https://jobs.netflix.com/work-life-philosophy)
- Rationale: the freedom exercises judgment, and it *"prevents the process creep that typically happens when companies grow and try to dummy proof their organizations."* (2024 memo)
- When trust breaks: one Taiwan employee was fired after an audit found $100k+ of personal vacations reimbursed over three years. (Variety) The rules are few — but enforcement is real.

## 8. How the culture shapes decisions

### 8.1 Willingness to kill projects / cancel shows
- Sarandos on greenlights: *"It's 70% gut and 30% data... Most of it is informed hunches and intuition. Data either reinforces your worst notion or it just supports what you want to do either way."* (via Forbes: https://www.forbes.com/sites/paultassi/2023/01/05/the-hidden-reason-netflix-keeps-cancelling-shows-like-1899/)
- Cost-vs-viewership renewal replaces longevity as the measure (Deadline: https://deadline.com/2019/03/netflix-tv-series-cancellations-strategy-one-day-at-a-time-1202576297/). Shows like 1899 and KAOS die on cliffhangers; the fan-facing cost is real (Newsweek: https://www.newsweek.com/netflix-keeps-canceling-shows-prematurely-why-1971080). Sarandos: *"I watch a lot of things that I'm glad we didn't make that I love 'cause I don't think a lot of people would've watched them."*

### 8.2 Candor as a control system — Qwikster (2011)
- 800,000 subscribers quit after the announced DVD spin-off; stock collapsed; Netflix reversed in ~3 weeks. The culture lesson Hastings drew: underlings had thought it was a bad idea and hadn't said so loudly enough → **"it is disloyal to Netflix when you disagree with an idea and do not express that disagreement."** (Variety). Sarandos still invokes Qwikster as lore for new joiners (WSJ, Oct 2024). Operational details: [experiments.md](experiments.md#31-qwikster-2011--the-most-famous-23-days-in-corporate-strategy).

### 8.3 Long-term orientation & tolerance of failure
- *"the things that are happening are never as bad as they seem, nor are they ever as good as they seem... let's just stay the course."* (on the 2022 crash, WSJ)
- *"You've gotta be willing to make hard sacrifices for the long-term business. And if you're petrified of those dips, you'll never do those things... Don't make the same mistake a thousand times, but go ahead and make a thousand mistakes and we'll be fine."*
- Fear culture vs learning culture: *"entertainment industry is mostly a fear culture... we intentionally did not go down that path."* (WSJ, Oct 2024)

### 8.4 Layoffs when the hard test fails
- June 2022: 300 employees cut (~3%): https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/digital/netflix-cuts-300-employees-in-new-round-of-layoffs-1235157991
- Oct 2026: reported ~5% workforce cut planned (https://variety.com/2026/tv/news/netflix-mass-layoffs-1236906567/) — announced the same season Netflix opened a larger São Paulo office for its 15-year Brazil anniversary (https://about.netflix.com/news/netflix-kicks-off-15-year-anniversary-celebrations-in-brazil-with-new-office). High-performance economics and market commitment coexist.

## 9. Culture evolving, 2023–2026

- **2024 memo rewrite:** new principle names; F&R heading retired; "brilliant jerks" line removed; keeper test retained; built with 1,500+ comments of "farming for dissent." (https://about.netflix.com/news/sharing-our-latest-culture-memo)
- **No companywide RTO mandate** — the industry outlier (Business Insider, Dec 2025: https://www.businessinsider.com/remote-work-media-companies-rto-wfh-paramount-nbcu-disney-netflix-2025-12), despite Hastings' 2020 remark that remote work was "a pure negative" (https://www.theverge.com/2020/9/8/21426956/netflix-ceo-reed-hastings-return-to-office-vaccine-working-from-home-negative).
- **AI doctrine:** *"I look at Gen AI as a tool for creators to create content, not for Netflix to create instead of creators... can this use of this tool make a better show?"* (Sarandos, WSJ 2024). First gen-AI final footage: the building-collapse VFX in Argentina's *El Eternauta* (July 2025), "finished ten times faster than traditional methods" (https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/netflix-says-it-used-genai-argentine-tv-series-2025-07-18/). Formal partner-facing GenAI rules published: https://studiopartner.netflix.net/studio/generative-workflows-applications-and-use-cases — plus a dedicated "Manager, Generative Workflows" role to turn experimentation into "repeatable, production-ready guidance."
- **Ads forced a new sub-culture:** *"we're building our ad teams from scratch, our sales teams from scratch. We're building new ad tech from scratch"* (Sarandos, WSJ, at ~40M ad-tier users). A 25-year-old consumer-subscription culture absorbing a B2B ad-sales culture, using "context not control" as the integration tool.
- **Co-CEO model as culture artifact:** *"our tech employees [must] feel like they work at the greatest tech company in the world. And our creative employees... the best entertainment company in the world. And they both have to be true."* Plus: *"there were three co-emperors in the Roman Empire. None of them went very well."* (WSJ, Oct 2024)

## 11. What this culture means for a new concept

Ideas that survive contact with this culture:

1. **Data-backed.** A concept must arrive as a testable hypothesis with an obvious first A/B test and a named metric (hours viewed, retention — not applause). "We believe users will love X" gets answered with "what does the test say?" (See [experiments.md](experiments.md), Pattern 1 & 8.)
2. **Candor-friendly.** Farm for your own dissent: name the strongest counter-argument and answer it. Overselling reads as naive in a room where withholding disagreement is formally disloyal. Expect the keeper-test question behind the question: *would I fight to keep this project?*
3. **Long-term.** They respect "hard sacrifices for the long-term business" and tolerate dips — a concept whose payoff is years out is credible *if* the learning loop starts immediately.
4. **Killable-scoped.** Frame it as an experiment that can die cheaply and quietly — one market, one format, one season — with a graceful fade designed in (Netflix never holds funerals; see [experiments.md](experiments.md), Pattern 7). "No longer a strategic priority" is how their experiments end; a new concept should be able to end that way too.
5. **Learning-not-fear framing.** The winning framing isn't "this will work" — it's "here's what we'll know in 90 days either way, and here's what Netflix learns even if it fails."
